Chapter 1

Motivation & Problem Statement

Intensity forecasting remains the hardest problem in tropical meteorology — and the ocean is the key missing piece.

🌀
2024 Season: Record Activity
79 named storms across all basins against a backdrop of record-warm SSTs. Notably active in all basins simultaneously.
📏
Scale-Blind Approaches
Most studies average over large spatial extents, obscuring mesoscale eddies and submesoscale fronts critical to intensity evolution.
🌡️
SST-Only Metrics Insufficient
Existing metrics (TCHP, MPI) rely on bulk SST or single-column profiles; neither accounts for dynamic sea level (MSLA) in a unified framework.
🔄
Full Life-Cycle Gap
Pre-formation, active, and post-dissipation oceanic co-evolution of SST & MSLA has not been characterized for a global multi-basin ensemble.

Research Goals

Objectives

1
Implement 2D Gaussian filter-based scale decomposition of GHRSST MUR SST and CMEMS MSLA into large-scale (>500 km), mesoscale (50–500 km), and submesoscale (<50 km) components for 78 cyclones.
2
Quantify scale-specific SST and MSLA anomalies during the pre-formation (5-day baseline), active cyclone, and post-dissipation temporal phases.
3
Develop and validate four COCI formulations — a novel composite metric integrating scale-decomposed ocean state with cyclone intensity (Vmax).
4
Conduct detailed case studies of Cyclone Alberto (Gulf of Mexico, June 2024) and Cyclone Dana (Bay of Bengal, October 2024).
5
Investigate pre-existing mesoscale ocean features (warm-core eddies, MSLA anomalies) as modulators of peak cyclone intensity.

2D Gaussian Scale Decomposition

The observed field F(x,y,t) is additively separated into three dynamically meaningful scales:

Large-Scale Flarge
> 500 km
σ ≈ 100 pts (SST)
σ ≈ 40 pts (MSLA)

Basin circulation, seasonal signal, geostrophic background
Mesoscale Fmeso
50–500 km
σ ≈ 10 pts (SST)
σ ≈ 4 pts (MSLA)

Energetic eddies, Rossby waves, frontal meanders
Submesoscale Fsub
< 50 km
Residual component
(SST only; MSLA resolution limited)

Fronts, filaments, upwelling

Temporal Phases & Cyclone-Ocean Coupling Index (COCI)

Three Temporal Phases
Pre-formation Baseline
5-day window before genesis · establishes background ocean state
Active Phase
Formation → landfall/dissipation · captures full cyclone–ocean interaction
Post Phase
5-day window post-dissipation · cold-wake recovery & geostrophic adjustment
ΔFscale = μactive − μbaseline
COCI Formulations
COCI-A (Equal-weight linear)
(ZSST + ZMSLA + ZV) / 3
COCI-B (Multiplicative ocean–atm.)
(ZSST + ZMSLA) × ZV
COCI-C (Correlation-weighted adaptive)
(wSST·ZSST + wMSLA·ZMSLA) / (wSST+wMSLA)
COCI-D (Ocean-only potential)
(ZSST + ZMSLA) / 2

Chapter 4 · Case Study 1

Cyclone Alberto · Gulf of Mexico · June 2024

First named storm of the 2024 Atlantic season · Slow-moving (~6 kt) · Landfall on Tamaulipas, Mexico

Pre-existing Ocean Conditions
🌡️  SST: 28–30°C — anomalously warm early-summer Gulf
📈  MSLA: +0.002–+0.003 m — Loop Current warm-core eddy
🔥  Large-scale SST anomaly: +0.8 to +1.2 K above baseline
🌀  Mesoscale warm patch: +0.6 K along NW-ward track
Scale-Decomposed Response (Active Phase)
Scale ΔSST (K) ΔMSLA (m)
Large-Scale −0.21 +0.008
Mesoscale −1.8 ± 0.4 +0.001
Submesoscale ±1.0–1.5 (asymm.) +0.002
⚡ Mesoscale cooling most intense (−1.8 K) due to slow translation (~6 kt) enabling prolonged wind forcing & deeper Ekman mixing. MSLA lag: 1–2 days.

Chapter 4 · Case Study 2

Cyclone Dana · Bay of Bengal · October 2024

Severe Cyclonic Storm · Faster-moving (~9 kt) · Landfall on Odisha coast, India

Pre-existing Ocean Conditions
🌡️  SST: 27–29°C — cooler than Gulf of Mexico (inter-basin asymmetry)
📉  MSLA: ≈ −0.1 m — shoaled thermocline; cyclonic eddy
⚖️  Large-scale SST anomaly: near-neutral +0.2 K
❄️  Mesoscale SST: modest −0.3 K cool anomaly pre-formation
Contrasts with Alberto
Mesoscale SST Cooling
Alberto: −1.8 K
vs
Dana: −1.2 K
Dana 33% weaker cooling → faster translation + shallower TCHP
Mesoscale MSLA Response
Alberto: −0.08 m
vs
Dana: −0.04 m
Half the MSLA response — limited warm-eddy structure available
🔑 Dana's negative pre-MSLA capped further intensification beyond Severe Cyclonic Storm; Alberto's warm-core eddy deepened the thermocline, enabling sustained intensification despite surface cooling.

COCI Performance — 78-Cyclone Ensemble

Index Pearson r p-value Significant?
COCI-A ★ 0.526 < 0.001 ✓ Yes
COCI-B 0.161 0.159 ✗ No
COCI-C 0.039 0.737 ✗ No

COCI-A validated against Vmax across n=78 cyclones spanning 25–145 kt. Two-tailed t-test, n−2 = 76 d.f. Basin-stratified coupling is strongest in NIO (r ≈ 0.65) and weakest in ENP (r ≈ 0.30).
r = 0.526
COCI-A vs Vmax · p < 10⁻⁶
Why COCI-A wins:
  • Empirical weights (COCI-C) are too weak — SST–Vmax r = 0.028, MSLA–Vmax r = 0.038
  • Multiplicative (COCI-B) doesn't outperform — coupling is additive, not non-linear at ensemble scale
  • Ocean-only (COCI-D) r ≈ 0 confirms ocean state is necessary but not sufficient without atmospheric co-variability
Cyclones over MSLA > +0.1 m (warm-core eddies) achieved peak winds 15–20 kt higher on average vs MSLA < −0.1 m

Chapter 5

Key Findings

🎯
Mesoscale dominates cyclone-induced cooling: Ensemble mean mesoscale SST anomaly = −0.62 ± 0.54 K; large-scale = −0.18 K; submesoscale highly heterogeneous & track-asymmetric.
🌊
Pre-existing MSLA modulates peak intensity: Storms over positive-MSLA (warm-core eddy) regions achieve Vmax 15–20 kt higher than those over negative-MSLA regions — consistent with enhanced TCHP and deepened 26°C isotherm.
📊
COCI-A achieves r = 0.526, p < 10⁻⁶ across the 78-cyclone global ensemble — first satellite-only, scale-aware, dual-variable coupling index validated at this ensemble scale.
🔬
Submesoscale SST gradient magnitude correlates with peak intensity (r = 0.32, p < 0.01) — intense cyclones generate stronger submesoscale frontal structures, implying enhanced air-sea enthalpy flux pathways.
⚡
Translation speed is a joint determinant: Alberto's ~6 kt translation drove 50% stronger mesoscale cooling than Dana's ~9 kt, despite similar intensity brackets — dwell time, not storm strength, governs cold-wake magnitude.

Contributions & Future Directions

Scientific Contributions
First global multi-basin, scale-decomposed analysis of both SST & MSLA for a full annual cyclone season
COCI framework: novel composite metric integrating dynamic MSLA + thermal SST in a scale-aware, satellite-only pipeline
Observational evidence for eddy-intensity coupling at global scale — previously validated only in individual case studies
Open-source Python pipeline (6 modules) — reproducible for any future season / alternative satellite datasets
